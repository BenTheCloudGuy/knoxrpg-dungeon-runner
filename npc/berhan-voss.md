# Berhan Voss

**Race:** Human
**Role:** The alchemist who built the Alchemical Hall and Alchemists Lab (Rooms 4-5), author of the potion recipes in [alchemists_workshop.md](../alchemists_workshop.md)

## Appearance

Late fifties. Thin from missed meals, not from any curse. Ink and acid burns on both hands, permanent, the fingertips of his left hand slightly discolored gray from years of handling reagents without gloves. He wore good clothes once. What's left of them is stained and patched. Reading spectacles, one lens cracked, still in his coat pocket if anyone finds his body or his effects.

## Personality / Voice Notes (for the journal)

Berhan was a working apothecary before he was anything else. Not a wizard, not a mad scientist. A tradesman who was good at his job and proud of precision. His journal voice should read like a man taking notes at his bench, not composing a memoir.

- Short, functional sentences. He writes to remember, not to be read.
- Numbers, measurements, and corrections. He crosses out his own mistakes in the text ("2 drops - no, 3. Fix this.").
- Dry, tired humor that shows up sideways, never as a punchline. He is not funny on purpose.
- He complains about the work before he complains about his captivity. The job comes first, even now.
- Entries get shorter and more erratic as the journal goes on. Early entries are full paragraphs. Late entries are fragments, missed dates, smudged ink.
- He never begs. He states facts, even ugly ones, plainly.
- He does not know he is writing a farewell. Nobody realizes that in the moment.

**Sample line (early journal):** *"Kingsfoil batch is weak again. Whoever is stocking this bench doesn't know the difference between fresh-cut and dried. Told him twice. He doesn't listen. He doesn't have to."*

**Sample line (mid journal, trade grievance):** *"Out of hart antler again. Used to mean a week's walk into goblin ground and back, hoping I came back with all my fingers. Since the little one set up his stall at the Drift Stones edge, it's a day, if the wild magic doesn't decide otherwise. Still better odds than the ground level. I'll take unpredictable over certain."*

**Sample line (late journal):** *"Ratio's wrong. Always wrong. Out of hart antler. Out of time, probably. Vat's louder tonight."*

## Final Journal Fragment

> Formula test. Full dose. 12 ml.
>
> I put it in the vat first. It held.
>
> Then it reacted. Wrong color. Too fast. The chemicals climbed the sides and came over my hands.
>
> I thought the formula had failed. It did not fail. It changed the work. It changed me.
>
> Skin gone gray? More than gray. Fingers too long. I can still write. The hand does not always wait for me.
>
> The vat chemicals are in my lungs. I smell them when I breathe. They are overtaking me. I know what exposure does.
>
> I am losing words. Losing the shape of a man. I can still count. I can still correct. That part is still here.
>
> Green. Need the Green Transmutation Crystal. It pulls. I need it.
>
> If I stop writing, I may stop being Berhan.

## Current Status / Fate

Berhan is a mutated creature roaming the dungeon halls. He hunts the Green Transmutation Crystal and attacks any player he sees carrying it. He is no longer in the Alchemist's Workshop.

The Alchemist's Workshop contains only the remaining workshop materials and the Green Transmutation Crystal at the bottom of the vat. Berhan is not there.

## Relationship to Xhal'theris and the Vault

Xhal'theris didn't build the workshop, Berhan did, under duress. He was taken off a trade road over a year before the current game's contestants ever arrived and kept alive because he was useful: he could produce potions, poisons, and "test material" for the Vault's trials on demand. Xhal'theris treated him the way he treats every specimen with a skill worth keeping, contempt wrapped around a use for him. Berhan was allowed a workbench, ingredients, and just enough dignity to keep working, in exchange for never being told when or if he'd be let go.

He wasn't loyal. He wasn't a collaborator by choice. He built the recipes because refusing meant the vat trial got tested on someone else instead, and he'd already decided that wasn't a bill he wanted on his hands. That's the whole of his politics. He never stopped trying to solve his way out of the room, he just ran out of time doing it the careful way.

## Supply Lines and Trade

Berhan didn't get his reagents delivered. He traded for them, with the Goblins in the caverns to the northeast of the Vault. Rare ingredients he couldn't grow or scavenge himself, like the hart antler powder and some of the stranger reagent stock, came from that trade.

For a long time this meant going all the way into Goblin Tribe territory himself, and he hated it. Not out of fear of the goblins specifically, he never wrote them up as the danger, but because it cost him whole days he needed for the bench. He resented the walk more than the company.

That changed when one of the Goblin Traders set up a small shop or stall near the entrance to the Drift Stones. It cut his trading time down considerably and he was, in his own understated way, grateful for it.

The Drift Stones themselves are not a safe shortcut. Wild magic rips through the whole region, unpredictable and random, and crossing it is a gamble every time. Berhan crossed it anyway, on the reasoning that it still beat the alternative: the ground level route, where Lava Elementals roam. To him it was a choice between two bad options, and the Drift Stones' odds, however strange, were better than a guaranteed elemental encounter. He never treated this as a brave choice. It was arithmetic, same as everything else on his bench.

## Hook

The party finds the Alchemical Hall and Lab as Berhan left them: bench mid-use, ingredients out, and his journal sitting open near the cauldron, last page torn. The recipe book (already in play) is his professional work. The journal is personal: it's where he actually is, in his own words, right up until he isn't. Reading it gives the party the same clue trail the workshop puzzle already provides, but framed as a dead man's notes instead of a printed manual, and it's the only place in the dungeon that says anything true about who built the Lab and why he stopped showing up to it.
