# The Prisoner's Letter

*(Read-aloud handout. Pass to a player. Aged parchment, ink faded in places, last paragraph written in a shakier hand than the first.)*

---

To whoever you are —

If you are reading this, then you have made it further than I did. Good. I hope you have a torch and friends still living. I had both, once. We were eight when we entered. We thought we knew what we were doing. We were guides, scouts, a cleric, a clever woman who could read a map better than anyone I'd ever met. We made it through more than I would have believed possible. We thought we had won when we reached the exit.

I want you to understand that. We stood at the gate. We saw the stairs going up. And then we saw what the gate required. Seven crystals! Seven of them, each a different color, set into seven sockets in the door. Without all seven, the gate does not open. So we turned around, and we went back in to find them. 

We split up and that was where we went wrong. We thought we could cover ground faster that way. We were wrong about that, too. One by one, the dungeon took them. Mara to the lake. Old Hen to the goblins. The cleric — I never found him. Just his blood on a wall and his sun-symbol kicked into a corner like an insult. I made it the longest. I think.

I believe I found one of the crystals. I found the Artificer's Cube — a thing of brass and color and gears, locked tight, with what I believe to be a crystal inside it. The cube would not open for me. <<MORE PENDING AFTER CUBE IS PRINTER>>

I was so tired. I had not slept in I don't know how long. I was eating cave moss. I heard things in the tunnels that may or may not have been there. I came into this room because I thought it was empty and I would be able rest for a brief period. I stepped wrong, because of exhaution or inattention I can't say. Well, the cage came down and the cage went up. That was 4 days ago.

If you are reading this, you have found my map. It is the best I could draw with what time I had left. I'm not as good as what Mara had on her when the creature in the Lake took her. There is a voice in this place. You may already have heard it. Do not believe it. Do not answer it! 

Find the crystals. All seven. Do not stop. Do not split up. 

DO NOT LET YOUR GUARD DOWN! 


If any of you make it out — please find my wife and children. Her name is Aelis, of the village of Brennmoor. Tell them I tried to come home to them. Tell them I am sorry.

Corwin Vale
